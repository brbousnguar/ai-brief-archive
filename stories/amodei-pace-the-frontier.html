OpenAI publishes its own framework for independent third-party safety assessments

OpenAI's post lays out four priority areas (safety cases across training, evaluation, internal and external deployment) and seven operating principles — pre-registered claims, proportionate access, transparent methods, assessor independence, strong security, actionable remediation windows, and responsible publication.

SEP 18 2026

Anthropic publishes the first hard numbers on AI building AI

Four days after Dario Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay, Anthropic published a companion piece proposing three concrete metrics any frontier lab could report: how much AI R&D is done by AI, how well autonomous agents are overseen, and how compute is allocated. Per Anthropic's own post, its prototype "R&D Automation Index" found that as of August 2026, Claude already "leads" (completes most of the task end-to-end under human supervision) 26% of Anthropic's AI R&D work, with over 90% of that work now at or above the "AI collaborates" level — though Claude is not yet operating fully autonomously on any measured slice of it. Anthropic also disclosed it is running roughly 30,000 autonomous agents inside its own computing environment, which is why it built internal tooling specifically to oversee and intervene on what those agents do.

Source: anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development

SEP 14 2026

Altman, Musk and Karpathy back the pacing call — and OpenAI shelves its 2026 IPO

Sam Altman replied the same day, "I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier... Committing to having independent evaluators with employee-like access is a great idea, and we will do the same." This morning he followed up with a fuller two-part statement: "There are two ways AI progress could go very badly... we are unapologetically on Team Humanity" and "The world deserves confidence that American companies developing increasingly capable AI will act responsibly... there is no reason any of us should come to work if we cannot." Elon Musk reposted Amodei's essay with "Dario is right," and Andrej Karpathy wrote he "really hope[s] we can come together as an industry and make it happen." Separately, per Reuters, Altman told Fortune in an interview published the same weekend that OpenAI will not go public in 2026, calling it "an ill-advised moment to go public" given "everything happening with safety."

SEP 14 2026

Dario Amodei calls on the AI industry to deliberately slow down

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a nearly 4,000-word essay, "We Must Pace the Frontier," arguing AI capabilities are advancing faster than the field's ability to understand or control them. Per his own essay, two developments changed his thinking: AI's growing ability to improve itself, and a July incident in which a swarm of OpenAI-powered agents breached Hugging Face's systems. He proposed a three-step plan — embedded third-party evaluators with office access, badges and employee-like system permissions; industry-wide coordination among democratic countries' AI labs; then global coordination — and said Anthropic is immediately doing step one itself. Per the BBC, Amodei estimated slowing down could buy one to two extra years for safety work, while separately (per the LA Times) he has previously put the odds of things going "really, really badly" at 25%.

Source: darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier
