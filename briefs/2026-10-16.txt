Meta ships an 816B open-weight model built to run on Mac Studio, and OpenAI/Anthropic fund mathematicians after a backlash over AI-generated papers

Meta and Apple partnered to get Meta's biggest open-weight model yet running locally on the new M5 Ultra Mac Studio, while OpenAI and Anthropic each put money into a Simons Foundation fund for mathematicians — a response to community anger after OpenAI flooded the field with AI-generated preprints.

01 · Meta releases Muse Spark Open, an 816B open-weight model, and partners with Apple to run it on Mac Studio
Per Meta's own announcement, Muse Spark Open is a mixture-of-experts model excelling at long-horizon tasks, complex software engineering and multi-step reasoning, available today via API platforms (Baseten, Fireworks AI, Groq, Modal, Nebius, OpenRouter, Together AI) and as downloadable weights (Hugging Face, Amazon SageMaker AI, Google Cloud Model Garden, Microsoft Foundry). It ships three days after Meta's closed, frontier-class Muse Wonder — suggesting Meta is running a fast release cadence to cover both the proprietary and open-weight ends of the market at once. NVIDIA noted the model was trained on its GB300 NVL72 systems.
Signal: Meta calls it "the most capable US open-weight model to date" — 816B total parameters (42B active), a 1M-token context window, natively multimodal, and trained specifically for agentic coding. The real hook is the Apple tie-in: it's the first model in its class built to run locally on two M5 Ultra Mac Studios via MLX, with Apple CEO John Ternus publicly congratulating the team — a notable thaw between two companies that have clashed for years.
Source: https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open

02 · OpenAI and Anthropic fund a $15M Simons Foundation effort to help mathematicians navigate AI-generated research
Per the Simons Foundation's own release, the $15M combined funding (from the foundation, Anthropic and OpenAI) will go toward an open call for proposals supporting workshops and initiatives that help researchers evaluate AI-assisted results, learn new tools, and shape collective standards for AI use in math — with both AI labs also providing compute and tools to Simons-supported researchers, extending beyond math into basic science generally.
Signal: this reads as a direct, fairly quick response to backlash — OpenAI reportedly released 722 AI-generated math manuscripts to the research community just over a week ago, upsetting mathematicians, and OpenAI's $5M contribution here (with Anthropic adding the rest alongside the foundation) looks aimed squarely at repairing that relationship rather than being a purely philanthropic move.
