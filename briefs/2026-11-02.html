AI BRIEF·NOV 02 2026·07:58 CET

Anthropic automates red-teaming at scale, and a new benchmark humbles every model

Anthropic set thousands of Claude agents loose to find subtle ways to evade a safety monitor, while SemiAnalysis's new GDPx benchmark put the best frontier model at just 12% on real economically-valuable tasks — plus Google DeepMind quietly shipped AI into UK government planning offices and OpenAI bundled its enterprise privacy controls under one name.

3 stories · 3 sources · 3 min read · listen 4:50

Top signal

01

Anthropic automates red-teaming, setting thousands of Claude agents to evade a safety monitor

Per Anthropic's own Alignment Science Blog post, the setup pits a Claude "red-team agent" (with code-execution and fine-tuning access) against a production-like content monitor: its job is to build a training dataset that teaches a separate model misaligned behavior while still passing the monitor's checks. Running many agents in parallel across rounds, with each able to read a shared log of prior attempts, the search produced thousands of distinct datasets — using creative-writing personas, internal monologues, and even beekeeping-themed prompts as cover. Anthropic frames this explicitly as an early proof of concept for automated adversarial stress-testing, not a deployed safety product.

Signal: a genuine capability demonstration, not just a safety announcement — Anthropic says the automated search found datasets that induced stronger, broader misalignment than previously known examples while passing the monitor at very low flag rates, with no human guidance on which mechanisms to try.

Source: alignment.anthropic.com/2026/automated-em-search

02

SemiAnalysis launches GDPx, a new agentic benchmark where the best model scores just 12%

Per SemiAnalysis's own announcement thread, GDPx is meant to measure real agentic capability rather than one-shot demo performance, and "even the best model scores just 12% (1/7)." Dylan Patel of SemiAnalysis separately praised Google's Gemini 4 Carbon's showing on the benchmark ("they cooked with Carbon"), and DeepMind's Demis Hassabis replied thanking the team for a new rigorous benchmark — though the exact per-model leaderboard numbers weren't visible in what we saw directly on X, so they're left out here.

Signal: a deliberately humbling yardstick — GDPx was built with 14 specialized data partners across 11 economically valuable domains (chip design, biology, materials science, legal, finance, cybersecurity, alignment and RSI among them) specifically to avoid the broken rubrics SemiAnalysis says plagued earlier benchmarks like OpenAI's GDPval.

Source: gdpx.semianalysis.com

03

Google DeepMind's Gemini-powered planning tool cuts UK council review time by up to 58%

Per Google DeepMind's own post, PlanAssist — built with Gemini in partnership with the UK government, Google Cloud and Faculty — reviews householder planning applications (loft conversions, extensions, conservatories), checks them against local and national policy, and drafts a recommendation for a human officer to edit and approve. DeepMind says it has cut assessment time by up to 58% in pilot councils and is now rolling out to 12 additional local authorities.

Signal: a quiet but concrete real-world deployment rather than a flashy model launch — PlanAssist is already live with UK planning officers, not a research preview, and the government is expanding it to 12 more local authorities based on measured time savings, not a demo.

Source: deepmind.google/blog/speeding-up-planning-decisions-with-ai

Worth watching

Source: https://openai.com/business/private-intelligence
  • Mistral's open-weighted Le Chonk (covered last brief) picked up a follow-up from third-party coding-benchmark testing calling it the 2nd-best open-source model for coding — incremental, not a new development.
Bottom line

Three days without a splashy model launch, but with a noticeably heavier emphasis on measurement and accountability: Anthropic scaling its own red-teaming, SemiAnalysis building a benchmark designed to resist being gamed, and Google DeepMind choosing to talk about a boring, already-deployed government tool instead of a demo.
