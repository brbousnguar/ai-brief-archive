Lean community certifies the AI-assisted Riemann Hypothesis proof as correct

The Riemann claim from Saturday's brief has moved from "unverified" to certified by the Lean and Mathlib communities, OpenAI's chief scientist published internal pretraining numbers and proposed shared safety limits, and Meta's chief AI officer warned humanity may not keep control of superintelligence.

01 · Riemann proof, day 3: the Lean community certifies it as correct
Running story: https://ai.heybrahim.com/stories/anthropic-claude-riemann-proof.html
Per the Lean community's own blog post, the Lean and Mathlib communities say they have "full confidence" that Levent Alpöge's proof is correct. They report that dozens of people compiled the library without errors, and that two independent tools confirmed the theorem proved is exactly Mathlib's statement of the Riemann Hypothesis. Scientific American reports mathematicians are now "almost certain" the proof is real. On the home feed, Elon Musk is pitching it as evidence of "super intelligence" and says it removes the conditional status of theorems that depend on the hypothesis. The Lean certification covers the formal statement and proof, so the open question is now reaction and credit, not whether it compiles.
Signal: Saturday's brief called this an unverified personal claim; that caveat no longer holds. The check that matters, that the Lean code proves exactly Mathlib's statement of the hypothesis, has been done by the community itself.
Source: https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis

02 · OpenAI's chief scientist publishes internal pretraining numbers and proposes shared safety limits
Jakub Pachocki, on his own account and in the OpenAI post "Perplexity and Intelligence," says OpenAI is giving more transparency into its pretraining progress "for the sake of moving toward shared safety bars." He argues for common limits based on perplexity and other measurements, with progress beyond them gated on being able to understand AI behavior. The post lists its own caveats: perplexity does not map directly onto tasks people care about, and it ignores gains from outside pretraining such as chain-of-thought reasoning. A commentator on the feed (Jacob Coxon) read the post as saying pretraining research is likely to be automated in 2027.
Signal: labs rarely disclose pretraining progress at all, so publishing it while arguing for common perplexity-based limits is a notable move. It is also a bet that the industry will accept one metric as a speed limit.
Source: https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence

03 · Meta's chief AI officer: "superintelligence is arriving," and the path is not inevitable
Per BigGo Finance's report on Alexandr Wang's post on X, he wrote that "it's hard to imagine a world where humans maintain control" over superintelligence far smarter than humans in every domain. Wang then followed up on his own account that much of this has been said by other lab leaders. He said he is saying the path is not inevitable, and that leadership matters more than ever. Jon Favreau quoted the original post, calling it "madness" and saying elected officials should do everything possible to stop the labs.
Signal: this is a Meta executive saying publicly that control is the open question. The feed reaction is political: Jon Favreau quoted it as a reason for elected officials to act against the frontier labs.
Source: https://finance.biggo.com/news/d96962f0-e86e-4a12-a00e-8e2e44e5773e
