Google DeepMind uses AI agents to formally prove Signal's crypto code secure

OpenAI opened free frontier-model access to up to 200,000 scientists worldwide and published data showing researchers nearly doubling their productivity with AI, while NVIDIA unveiled an open model family built for chemistry and materials-science agents.

3 stories · 3 sources · 3 min read · listen 4:29

Top signal

01

Google DeepMind uses AI agents to formally prove Signal's crypto code secure

Per Google DeepMind's own announcement (and CEO Sundar Pichai's account), researchers used a new framework called AlphaProof Code to mathematically verify libsignal, the reference implementation behind the Signal messaging protocol, down to compiled machine code. Agent teams powered by Gemini 4 Carbon generated more than 6 million lines of machine-checked Lean proofs, with specifications co-developed with Claude Opus 5.5 and reviewed by humans; about 1% of the codebase needed human review, with Lean automatically checking the rest. Five researchers directed the work over four weeks in October. Signal's CTO Ehren Kret is quoted saying it gives the company "even greater confidence" in its privacy guarantees.

Signal: this is a genuine jump in what AI-assisted formal verification can do at scale — turning years of expert manual proof work into weeks, with almost no human-written proof code, and open-sourcing the result for the security community to check.

Source: deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code

02

OpenAI expands free ChatGPT access to 200,000 scientists, publishes productivity data

Per OpenAI's own announcement, the ChatGPT for Academic Researchers program is expanding to give up to 200,000 scientists, mathematicians and engineers worldwide free access to its frontier models, with applications opening November 23; OpenAI says it does not use participants' data to train its models by default. Separately, OpenAI's own account said its Economic Research team studied more than 1,800 participants in the pilot and found they reported nearly doubling their productivity using AI in their work.

Signal: OpenAI is scaling a program it started with 10,000 researchers in July to 20x that size, and backing it with its own usage data rather than just a pledge — a sign it sees scientific research as a priority wedge, not just a goodwill program.

Source: openai.com/index/expanding-chatgpt-for-academic-researchers

03

NVIDIA unveils Nemotron for Science, an open model family for lab agents

Per NVIDIA's own blog and its AI account (announced by VP Ian Buck at the SC26 supercomputing conference), Nemotron for Science pairs an open-weights 120-billion-parameter multimodal model, Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, with domain-specific training recipes and a new open chemistry-reasoning benchmark, LitmusBench, to help research labs build agents for chemistry, materials science and molecular biology.

Signal: NVIDIA is positioning this specifically for autonomous research loops — agents that plan, simulate, check and adjust — rather than another general chatbot benchmark entry.

Source: blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news
