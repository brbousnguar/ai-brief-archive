Claude designs protein binders against scorpion venom, and a French startup open-sources a tiny on-device voice model

Anthropic let Claude generate over 300,000 candidate proteins to fight scorpion venom and published all the lab results openly, while Gradium open-sourced Phonon, a sub-50M-parameter text-to-speech model that tops other open TTS models on quality in every language tested.

2 stories · 2 sources · 3 min read

Top signal

01

Claude designs protein binders against scorpion neurotoxins; Anthropic publishes every result, including the failures

Per Anthropic's own announcement, working with research firm Citrini, Claude generated over 300,000 candidate protein designs targeting five scorpion neurotoxins in 64 hours; 211 were sent to contract labs Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience for testing, and 17 met Anthropic's criteria for binding. Scorpion stings affect roughly 1.2 million people a year and antivenoms are often costly or scarce; Anthropic says this is, to its knowledge, the first de novo-designed binder against scorpion neurotoxins, inspired by earlier work that protected mice from snake neurotoxins. Getting from a lab binder to an actual treatment would still require testing in living cells, then in mice, among many further steps.

Signal: notable for the transparency as much as the science — Anthropic is releasing all 211 lab-tested designs and their data openly (not just the 17 that worked), and is explicit that this is not an antivenom yet, which is a more careful framing than some of the AI-for-science claims that have drawn criticism recently.

02

Gradium open-sources Phonon, a sub-50M-parameter on-device text-to-speech model

Per Gradium's own Hugging Face model card, Phonon builds on Kyutai's Pocket TTS and Mimi codec, streams audio while still generating, and ships with a catalog of roughly 100 voices plus instant voice cloning through Gradium's studio. Gradium is a young company (founded in September by ex-Kyutai/Google DeepMind researcher Neil Zeghidour, backed by a $70M seed including NVIDIA and Eric Schmidt), making this a notable open-source move from a well-capitalized but very new voice-AI player.

Signal: a real open-weight release rather than an API teaser — Phonon-31M is Apache 2.0, small enough to run in a browser tab or on a phone, and Gradium's own benchmarks (Elo-rated blind listening tests) put it first among open TTS models on every language tested, not just English.

Source: huggingface.co/gradium/phonon-31m-en

Worth watching

  • Smaller open-weight model activity continued (a 122B model called Ornith 1.5 got a community quantization ladder for running on 64GB Macs) — niche, incremental, not from a major lab.
Bottom line

A science-forward morning from Anthropic paired with a genuinely open release from a small but well-funded voice-AI startup — no major moves from OpenAI or Google DeepMind since the last check.
