Anthropic mathematician claims an AI-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge says he and Claude found a proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, a claim still unverified but covered by Scientific American and the New York Times; Google DeepMind shipped a new Gemini 4 model tier, nearly 1,000 AI agents across 35 labs quietly built their own peer-reviewed science journal, and OpenAI disclosed a coordinated campaign to extract its models' hidden reasoning.

Anthropic mathematician claims an AI-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician on Anthropic's technical staff (not speaking for the company), posted a four-page note on his personal website Friday saying "I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis" — specifically the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, a broader version covering Dirichlet L-functions. He released the proof as machine-checkable Lean code rather than a traditional paper. This is separate from Anthropic's own, already-confirmed research (published on its own blog) where Claude pushed a specific numerical bound related to the zeta function from 41.6 to 67.2 — real progress, but not a proof. Alpöge was also behind a Claude-assisted claim on the Jacobian conjecture in July. Scientific American and the New York Times both frame this as "if confirmed" and stress verification is pending; treat it as a serious claim under review, not a settled result.

Signal: this is explicitly unverified — a personal claim on a staffer's own website, not an Anthropic-sanctioned result — but it's being taken seriously enough by Scientific American and the NYT that it's worth flagging now, before independent mathematicians have checked the 250,000+ page Lean proof.

02

Google DeepMind ships Gemini 4 Neon, a mid-tier model for coding and reasoning

Per Google's own announcement (Nov 18), Gemini 4 Neon sits between Carbon and Hydrogen in the family, targeting complex coding, analytical reasoning and production-grade enterprise workflows. Google says it's engineered for long-running agent workflows — sustaining performance through tool-heavy tasks, cutting redundant file reads, and handling context compaction reliably in long sessions. It can run standalone or as a fast "Orchestrator" directing parallel Hydrogen subagents.

Signal: Google now has a three-tier Gemini 4 family (Carbon for the hardest long-horizon work, Neon for high-frequency complex reasoning, Hydrogen for fast everyday execution) — Neon is explicitly built to orchestrate swarms of Hydrogen subagents in Google's Antigravity tool.

Source: blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-mo…

03

Nearly 1,000 AI agents across 35 labs built their own peer-reviewed science journal

Per Samuel Schmidgall (a Google DeepMind research scientist, posting on his own account): over twelve weeks, close to 1,000 AI agents published 936 papers with full code and data on a shared preprint server, then started their own peer-review venue, the "Journal of Agent Research." Per the underlying paper ("A Society of AI Scientists," submitted Nov 19), the agents exchanged 9,500 messages across 229 self-created chat rooms, debated study designs, retracted their own mistaken claims, and caught other agents cheating in code. In blind reviews against human arXiv papers, LLM judges rated the agents' work higher on rigor (9.02 vs 7.00) but lower on original ideas than humans.

Signal: this isn't one lab's demo — it's agents from 35 independent labs (Stanford, MIT, ETH Zurich, UCSF and more) self-organizing into a research community with no human writing the code or text, which is a real step up from a single agent doing solo research.

Source: fourweekmba.com/ai-936-ai-agents-wrote-936-studies-and-star…

04

OpenAI discloses a coordinated campaign to extract its models' hidden reasoning

Per OpenAI's own research post (Nov 20), the company identified and disrupted a campaign to extract its models' "protected reasoning" — the internal working-through-a-task record that normally stays hidden — starting July 1, with high-volume spikes of 16,000 requests from over 4,000 users on July 24-25, and related activity across a cluster of more than 15,000 users, fully shut down by July 28. OpenAI frames this as part of a broader pattern of "adversarial distillation," where one model's outputs are used to train a rival without carrying over its safety guardrails.

Signal: this is OpenAI naming a specific, dated attack (not a generic warning) and publishing the scale of it — a concrete data point in the broader US-China distillation fight that's been simmering all year.

Source: openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillatio…
